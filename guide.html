Designer's Reference Guide

How Skill Prism Works

A plain-language guide to what the app does, how to use it, and what's happening under the hood — no code knowledge required.

The Big Idea

A topic exploder powered by AI

You type in anything you want to learn — a skill, a subject, a weird question — and the app breaks it into 8 sub-topics. Click any of those, and it breaks that into 8 more. Keep drilling and you build a layered map of the territory.

The word "fractal" in the name means the same pattern repeats at every level: big topic → 8 parts → click one → 8 smaller parts → click one → 8 even-smaller parts. You can go as deep as you want.

The AI part: Every breakdown is generated live by Claude (Anthropic's AI). Nothing is pre-written. Ask about topology, ask about sourdough — the app handles it.

Using the App — Step by Step

1

Type anything in the box

The start screen has a single text box. Type a topic and press Enter, or click one of the example links below the box.

The examples are randomized — they change each time you load the page. They're meant to show you the range of things that work: serious academic subjects, practical skills, abstract concepts, silly questions. Anything goes.

2

Wait a moment while the AI generates the breakdown

The grid appears progressively — cells fill in from left to right as Claude streams the response. You'll see the rows arrive one by one. This takes a few seconds.

Why is it streaming in? The app is receiving the AI's response in real-time, word by word, and painting cells as data arrives. This is faster than waiting for everything to finish before showing anything.
3

Read the grid

The main view is an 8×8 grid. The top row (dark cells) are the 8 main sub-topics. The columns below each one show 8 concrete components of that sub-topic.

4

Click a cell to drill deeper

Clicking any cell generates a new breakdown for that specific topic, interpreted in the context of where you started. The drill-in is context-aware: "Kerning" under Typography will be different from a hypothetical "Kerning" reached through a different path.

The framing (italic text) is a one-sentence take on what this topic really asks of you — not a definition, but a practitioner's perspective.

The first moves are 3 specific, real resources: books, courses, people to follow, or sites to bookmark. Clicking a book title opens a Goodreads search. Clicking a site or person opens their page.

Clicking the "i" icon on any cell in the grid loads that cell's insight in the right panel, without drilling into it. Useful for previewing what a sub-topic contains before committing to a drill.

The URL — Your Position is Shareable

The URL updates as you drill

Every time you drill into a topic, the browser's address bar updates. That URL captures exactly where you are — you can copy and share it, and anyone who opens it will land at the same spot in the same breakdown.

URL localhost:5179/#Typography/Spacing/Kerning
Refresh-safe: Reloading the page won't lose your place. The path is in the URL, and the app will regenerate the breakdown automatically. It may take a moment because it's asking the AI again.

Glossary of Terms Used in the UI

Breakdown
The AI-generated set of 8 main topics + 8 sub-topics each for any given focus. One breakdown = one screen's worth of grid.
Fractal View
The main 8×8 grid you interact with. Called "fractal" because the same pattern repeats at every drill level.
Path
The chain of topics you've drilled through. Shown as a breadcrumb trail. Example: Typography → Spacing → Kerning.
Insight Pane
The right-side panel on desktop that shows a framing statement and 3 learning resources for the current focus topic.
Framing
A one-sentence practitioner's take on what the topic requires you to understand. It's AI-written, styled like advice from someone who's deep in the field.
First Moves
The 3 specific resources (books, courses, people, or sites) shown in the Insight panel. Each one is a concrete starting point.
Depth
How many levels deep you've drilled. Depth 1 = just the topic you typed. Depth 3 = you've drilled twice from that starting point.
Pinned Term
When you click the "i" icon on a cell, that cell's insight is "pinned" in the right panel — it shows that cell's resources instead of the current focus.

What the AI is Actually Doing

Two separate AI calls happen

The app makes two kinds of AI requests, and they're independent:

1 — Grid generation: When you type a topic and hit Enter, the app asks Claude to decompose it into 8 mains and 8 subs each. The AI acts as a "curriculum designer" and is told to keep labels short (1–2 words), specific, and ordered from foundational to advanced.
2 — Insight generation: When the right panel loads for a term, a second AI call generates the framing sentence and 3 resources. This one asks Claude to think like a "thoughtful practitioner" recommending resources to a curious friend — not the most-famous textbook answers, but what someone deep in the field would actually suggest.

Both calls are cached within a session — if you drill back to a topic you've already visited, the existing result loads instantly without hitting the AI again.

App Layout at a Glance

Top bar
The header row. On the left: the Skill Prism logo (click to reset). In the middle: breadcrumb trail of your current path. On the right: export button, panel toggle.
Main grid
Center of the screen. The 8×8 breakdown. Dark top row = main categories. Lighter cells below = specific components. Click any cell to drill in.
Right panel
Desktop only. Insight pane for the current focus, or Export panel when you've clicked the export button. The panel can be hidden with the toggle in the top bar.
Corner cells
The empty cells in the four corners of the screen grid. They're part of the layout's 3×3 outer frame — purely structural, not interactive.
